Royal Academy of the Arts

Early in my career, I had the opportunity to help the Royal Academy of Arts design, build, and launch a new website. The project was an ambitious effort to showcase the Academy’s exhibitions, events, and cultural programming with the same level of care, creativity, and visual impact reflected in the art itself.

The design was bold, distinctive, and highly detailed. Every aspect of the experience needed to align closely with the Academy’s established visual identity, which was supported by a comprehensive and exacting set of brand guidelines. This required a high level of precision throughout the design and development process, where even small details could affect the integrity of the final experience.

Our role was to translate the Academy’s vision into a polished, responsive digital experience while preserving the intent of the original design. We worked closely with the Academy to ensure that the user experience, visual language, and interaction details were faithfully carried through to the web-creating an experience that felt as considered and distinctive as the institution itself.

Working closely with the Royal Academy’s visual designers and brand specialists, we designed and built a custom content management system using Ruby on Rails. Rather than adapting the Academy’s workflows to an off-the-shelf platform, we created a CMS tailored to the way its teams planned, created, and published exhibitions and events.

The platform gave content editors the flexibility to build new exhibition and event pages independently. They could upload and curate artwork, manage page content, and select visual treatments - including page and typography colors - without relying on developers for routine updates.

At the same time, the CMS included thoughtful guardrails that protected the integrity of the Academy’s visual identity. The system prevented incompatible color combinations and constrained design choices to approved, accessible options. This allowed editors to create expressive, visually distinctive pages while maintaining brand consistency and supporting accessibility standards.

The result was a publishing experience that balanced creative freedom with structure, giving the Academy greater control over its digital content without compromising usability, accessibility, or the quality of the visitor experience.

The new website launched to significant enthusiasm from the Academy, with overwhelmingly positive feedback from users. Beyond the success of the initial release, the platform gave the Royal Academy the flexibility to evolve its digital presence over time. Its custom CMS allowed the team to adapt content, refine visual treatments, and support new exhibitions and events without sacrificing the consistency or quality of the experience.

A key goal of the project was to make the website feel like a natural extension of the Academy itself. Browsing the site was designed to evoke the experience of stepping into the museum-immersive, visually expressive, and thoughtfully curated.

Founded in 1768, the Royal Academy brought centuries of artistic heritage to the project. Helping translate that legacy into a modern digital experience was both a rewarding responsibility and an enjoyable design challenge. The result was a platform that honored the institution’s history while giving it the flexibility and tools to continue evolving in the digital age.
