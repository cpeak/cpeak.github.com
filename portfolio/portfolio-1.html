Agvance SKY Platform Rebuild

In the fall of 2022, I joined SSI as a Senior Designer to help modernize Agvance, a legacy Windows-based enterprise platform, by transitioning its capabilities to Agvance SKY - a modern, web-based suite of applications.

My work focused on Grain, a core application used by farmers and merchandisers to manage the complex lifecycle of grain contracts, including purchasing, selling, and transportation. The project involved translating established workflows from a mature desktop system into an intuitive, scalable web experience while preserving the depth and reliability that users depended on in their day-to-day operations.

As a Senior Designer, I partnered with product managers, engineers, and subject-matter experts to understand existing workflows, identify opportunities to improve usability, and create experiences that supported the needs of both farmers and grain merchandisers.

Understanding the business model and gaining trust

The first challenge was developing a deep understanding of the grain business model. Moving grain from the farm to food manufacturers is a complex orchestration of people, logistics, pricing, and contracts. With many interconnected workflows and stakeholders, gaining a complete picture of the system initially felt like drinking from a firehose.

The second challenge was building trust with SSI and helping the team move beyond a feature-parity mindset. The initial goal was largely to replicate the existing Windows application in a web-based platform, with limited focus on improving the user experience.

To create meaningful value, I needed to demonstrate that modernization was more than translating screens from one platform to another. It was an opportunity to reevaluate established workflows, reduce complexity, and design a more intuitive experience around the needs of farmers and grain merchandisers.

Identifying areas of opportunity

Once I had developed a stronger understanding of the business and its workflows, I began identifying opportunities to improve the existing experience. Many behaviors in the legacy application were shaped by technical limitations, database requirements, or individual customer requests rather than by the broader needs of users. This created an opportunity to distinguish between constraints that were still relevant and legacy patterns that could be reconsidered.

To better understand how the product was used in practice, I conducted interviews with SSI’s customers and gathered firsthand insights into the real-world needs, goals, and challenges of its user base. These conversations helped ground design decisions in user behavior and business context rather than relying solely on the workflows established in the legacy application.

At the same time, SSI had developed a visual component library and was beginning to adopt Figma as part of its design process. Designers used the library to create wireframes and interface designs that developers would then implement. This provided a foundation for greater consistency and created an opportunity to establish a more collaborative, scalable workflow between design and engineering.

Working with their customers

SSI’s strong relationships with its customers gave our team direct access to the people using Agvance in their day-to-day work. This allowed us to involve customers throughout the design process - not only when developing new features, but also when improving existing workflows.

I created clickable prototypes and facilitated feedback sessions with customers to evaluate proposed solutions in a realistic, interactive format. These sessions helped us understand how users interpreted the experience, identify points of confusion, and validate whether the designs supported their goals and workflows.

Following each round of feedback, the team synthesized the research, identified recurring themes and opportunities, and incorporated those insights into the next design iteration. We continued this cycle of prototyping, testing, and refinement until we had sufficient confidence that the solution addressed user needs while supporting the broader goals of the product.

Working with their developers

Before I joined the team, the typical workflow ended when designers handed wireframes to developers and moved on to the next story. There was limited front-end QA or design validation after handoff, making it difficult to ensure that the implemented experience accurately reflected the intended design.

Consistency was also a growing challenge across the Agvance SKY suite. Some teams continued to rely on older components or created one-off solutions, resulting in fragmented patterns and an inconsistent experience between applications.

To address these issues, we restructured the design-to-development workflow and brought developers into the design process earlier. By involving engineering in design discussions, we were able to identify technical considerations sooner, build shared ownership of the experience, and reduce ambiguity before implementation began.

Designers also became more involved after handoff, reviewing implemented work and partnering with developers to resolve gaps between the design intent and the final product. This created a stronger feedback loop, improved consistency across the suite, and helped ensure that the shipped experience met our quality standards.

Building something better

Over the course of the project, we transformed more than the product - we evolved how teams collaborated to design and deliver it. By bringing design and development together earlier, strengthening implementation quality, and grounding decisions in direct customer feedback, we broke down organizational and technical barriers that had limited the previous workflow.

The result was a more cohesive, modern suite of applications that improved upon the legacy experience rather than simply replicating it. Customer involvement throughout the process helped us validate our decisions, refine key workflows, and build confidence that the platform would meet real-world needs.

I transitioned off the project as SSI began providing early access to a select group of its most trusted customers. Hearing the overwhelmingly positive feedback that followed was a rewarding validation of the team’s work. We had created a product that customers were eager to use - and a suite of applications the team was proud to put its name behind.
