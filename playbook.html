Design Playbook

How does a team go from an idea to a launched product online successfully? How do they do it on time, or under budget, or feature complete all while maintaining your sanity? I've been a designer for 15 years and I've been on more projects than I can count. I want to collect some of the wisdom I've accumulated over the years and put them together in a sort of Design Playbook - something to follow and keep in mind when kicking off and working through software development projects.

Layout a Project Charter

Create a document that lives in the repo, accessible to everyone, that defines core goals, rough project scope, MVP, stakeholders, and more importantly document ethical concerns. What is the sprint cadence? How does the team feel about working more than 40 hours a week? How do you handle disagreements? Tabs vs spaces. These things need to be mapped out and able to be referred to at anytime. Sign your names to it and make sure everyone sticks to it.

Scrutinize deadlines

Does the project have a deadline? Why was this chosen? Is it an arbitrary date to hit handed down by the C-Suite? A go-live date picked out of a hat? Pressure from investors? Now is the time to really push back and ensure milestone dates are set in a reasonable and meet the work/life balance of the team.

Pre Post-Mortem

Imagine yourself a year from now - what do you imagine went well on the project? Or conversely what doomed it? This is purely a theoretical exercise, but when addressing major projects concerns time is essential.

Uncover the real problem

What are you actually building here?

All hands as early as possible

Get devs and QA in as early as possible - if there are rocks to break down we want to know asap.

Hard stop: Are we all on board?

Get the charter out, and make sure everyone at the table agrees with the plan. Now is the time to bring up any lingering concerns or things that may have not been addressed yet. The key point here is to make sure everyone feels good moving forward. Treat it like NASA's Mission Control room in Houston - go around the horn and get a Go/No Go from each team. Hopefully with much fewer cigarettes.

Not time for solutions

The kickoff and exploration phase is not the time for solutions, its the time for finding the real problems to solve, framing them, and prioritizes them. It's so tempting to solve things right off the bat, but when you're building a foundation you need to do it right.

If your team is constantly putting out fires, the solution isn't to hire more firefighters - you need to figure out why the fires are starting in the first place
